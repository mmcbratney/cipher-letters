A tenor for London: Count Starhemberg to Giuseppe Riva, December 1720 to June 1721

Three letters from Vienna to the Duke of Modena's agent in London: keep writing in cipher, a return to England as Ambassador, and the tenor Borosini for a London opera company. Most of the cipher was deciphered at the time between the lines. What is new is the part with no gloss, read in part with the key taken from that decipherment.

What is new and what was known

Most of this cipher was deciphered at the time. 267 of the 426 cipher units carry a gloss written on the letters themselves: at the foot of c. 17r, and between the lines on cc. 1 and 13v. Every key value used here comes from that contemporary decipherment. 234 of the glossed units are read here. Not ours.

The new part is the 159 units with no gloss, of which 138 are read. No claim of a first reading is made even for those. Lowell Lindgren's article on Riva (Il Saggiatore musicale 23/2, 2016, pp. 277-374) was not seen: it is not open. At second hand it uses this Starhemberg file and treats Riva's recruiting of singers. The Borosini passage of 14 Jun 1721 is exactly its subject and is mostly glossed on the page, so it is likely to be quoted there. Treat that letter as probably known until the article is read. Lindgren and Timms (2003) was searched in an open copy: no cipher, and these letters are not printed there.

What this work adds: transcriptions of the three letters, the key taken from the gloss and carried to the unglossed parts, readings and English translations with every hole marked. Two of the letters are not tagged as cipher in the library's catalogue.

The letters

Three letters from Count Starhemberg in Vienna to Giuseppe Riva, the Duke of Modena's agent in London. The library files them under Guido von Starhemberg. The content fits Conrad Sigmund von Starhemberg: on 31 May 1721 he writes "il mio ritorno in Inghilterra ... con carattere d'Ambasciatore". The reviewer accepts this.

This is not the cipher of Riva's later letters from Selvatico, Olivazzi and Starhemberg (the Riva table, on its own page). That table, applied to the 31 May 1721 letter, gives no language.

What the letters say

Italian as read; English from the translations in the repository. [?word] = a code value or a word read by sense, not counted read; [85] = a code with no value; [...] = not read; a letter in brackets = supplied or emended.

21 December 1720: a postscript

"Lei continui di scrivermi in [z]iffere spesso.""Do continue to write to me in [?cipher] often."

The first line is glossed; "in ziffere spesso" is not.

31 May 1721: a return as Ambassador

His return to England is certain, as Ambassador. In cipher: the Duke of Modena does well to continue his devotion to the House of Austria (glossed). The rest is not glossed and is new:

"tanto più che gli essempi mostrano che [q]uelli [?che] sono stati fedeli à quella hanno ac[qu]istati grandissimi vantaggi""the more so as the examples show that [?those] [?who] have been faithful to it have [?gained] very great advantages"

He will say more on this point when he arrives.

14 June 1721: Borosini

He asks about the South Sea affair, and whether an opera company will be formed in London this year and what it would pay an excellent singer. Then:

"In tal caso sarebbe qui [B]orosini, orator(?) eccellente che canta il tenore e non hà suo parec[...]io in tutta Europa.""In that case there would be here [?Borosini], an excellent orator(?) who sings tenor and has not his [?equal] in all Europe."

Borosini would join if asked for in the name of the King or of the company; a letter of request to Starhemberg would do. A certain Colman has arrived to serve under St Saphorin: is he the son of "Hoffman"? And Starhemberg wants the talk of court and nation in London about Vienna's conduct towards "Sig. Cnigt" (Robert Knight, the South Sea cashier, by sense).

New, with no gloss: "suo parec[...]io", the proposal and "servirò ... il tenore Borosini", "il St. Saporin", "al sig. [Hoffman]", "toccante il sig. Cnigt", the closing words and the signature code.

The cipher

One number per letter, separated by dots. Vowels are 1-5 (a e i o u) and again 10 20 30 40 50. Consonants run in alphabetical order from 11: b c d f g l m n p q r s t z are 11 to 24. A double stroke after a group doubles the consonant. Numbers mostly from 80 up are codes for common words and names (80 questo, 84 et, 86 essere, 89 che, 94 lo, 96 di, 97 della, 98 di, 108 Hoffman, 210 Duca: all from the glosses).

One number gave trouble. In the key 20 is e, but 20 also stands where q is wanted (quelli, qui, acquistati). The order of the table explains it: 20 is the second vowel among the tens and the tenth consonant in the run from 11, so the writer's table had 20 twice. This is derived, not seen in a key sheet, and no count uses it. As the reviewer notes, the cipher resembles the 1719 table of targets/selvatico1719, which has 20 = qu, but r s t z differ.

How it was read

  1. 11 Nov 2026. A ciphertext-only solver run on the 31 May 1721 letter (104 groups) gave no reading. The gloss at the foot of the page had not been noticed.
  2. The Riva table was tried on the 31 May letter as a control: 597 of 5,000 shuffled keys did as well. Not that table.
  3. Key K21 (22 letter values and 210 = Duca) was taken from the contemporary gloss at the foot of c. 17r, which covers the first half of the 31 May letter and breaks off in mid-word. Applied to the second half, which has no gloss: 31 of 36 units.
  4. A sweep of the Starhemberg file found two more letters in this cipher (14 Jun 1721 and 21 Dec 1720), both partly glossed between the lines. Their glosses were not used to build the key.
  5. K21 was applied once, unchanged, to each: 14 Jun 1721, 249 of 289 units; 21 Dec 1720, 31 of 33 as first counted, 30 of 33 after one correction.
  6. 12 Nov 2026. After Daniel Bourdeau's check, the table-order explanation of 20 was worked out. One crop of the 31 May letter was looked at for the form of 20; the full-scale recheck was not done.

Checks

  • Shuffled keys: 14 Jun 1721, 0 of 5,000 shuffled keys as good.
  • Statistical validation: the repository's test gives "pass" (z 5.7 against random keys).
  • Maintainer's check: Daniel Bourdeau confirmed the 31 May figures (104 units, 93 read, 31 of 36 without a gloss). The two letters of the issue comment could not be checked without files.
  • Integration check: the build script reproduces every count. A second decoder written from the key and transcriptions alone agrees on 33 of 33, 104 of 104 and 282 of 289 groups (the seven differences are trailing doubt marks and the sign for an illegible group). An AI reader compared all 104 groups of c. 17r with the image: the transcription agrees throughout.
  • Time stamps: RFC 3161 stamps of 11 Nov 2026 hold the key file at 22:07:00 GMT (31 May), 23:09:47 (21 Dec) and 23:29:11 (14 Jun). They show only that the files existed then; that the key was fixed before the other two letters were transcribed is by the contributor's record.

What remains uncertain

  • Every 20 needs a re-read at full scale (e in "verso", "essempi"; q in "quelli", "qui"). In "acquistati" the table-order reading leaves 23 to be u in one place and t in the others.
  • Codes 59, 9, 62, 85, 236 and 301, and four places of the 14 Jun letter, are open.
  • The glosses of 14 Jun and 21 Dec are not transcribed diplomatically, and the clear text of the letters mostly not. Those two transcriptions were not compared with any image by anyone but their transcriber.
  • One transcription only, not blind (the key and the glosses were visible). The 1720 letter is 33 units, too short to say much.
  • cc. 3-12 and 15-16 of the Starhemberg file were not examined for more of this cipher.
  • Lindgren 2016 not seen, so priority is not established; the Borosini passage is the most exposed.

Who did what

The key is the contemporary decipherment written on the letters in 1721: every key value comes from it, and it covers 267 of the 426 cipher units. Malcolm McBratney (Brisbane) contributed the work, with Claude (Anthropic) sessions finding the letters, transcribing, taking the key from the gloss, reading and checking. Daniel Bourdeau checked issue #432 on 12 Nov 2026, raised the question of 20 and q, asked for this cipher to have its own entry apart from the Riva table, and accepts that the writer is Conrad Sigmund, not Guido.

Claude sessions found the letters, transcribed, took the key from the gloss, read and checked. No reading by a person without AI. There is no second transcription and no second reader of the three letters; the one image comparison (c. 17r) was made by an AI reader. No person has compared any transcription with the images.
