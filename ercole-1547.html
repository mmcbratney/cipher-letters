"The ladder of Spain into Italy": Ercole II d'Este to Claudio Ariosti, 8 January 1547

The Duke of Ferrara tells his secretary, then on a mission to Germany, to find out all he can about the rising at Genoa. Eight lines of the letter are in a sign-and-vowel cipher of the Este chancery. The key was recovered here from the ciphertext alone. No earlier key or reading was found in the places searched.

What is new and what was known

Before any transcription the contributor searched the pages and the Estense record, Cyphersolver's catalogue entry 2160 and its target folders, the DECODE database, the web, the inventory of the Modena State Archive's Germany embassy files (first half only) and a study of Este ciphers that covers the keys of 1495 and 1512. No key, decipherment or printed reading of this letter was found. Seven neighbouring Este keys were compared; none fits.

Brice Dudout tried this letter first, on 5 November 2026: he transcribed the eight lines, tried the 1559 key and its neighbours, and declared it not solved (Cyphersolver, targets/ariosto1559).

Not checked: the Este chancery's cipher books in the Modena State Archive, where the original key may survive; Claudio Ariosti's own embassy file there (busta 7); P. Cremonini's Segrete scritture estensi (2019, in print); full-text searches in HathiTrust and Google Books. Not finding a reading is not proof that none exists.

What this work adds: a key recovered from the cipher itself, one frozen decode of all eight lines, an Italian reading with every hole marked, and an English translation.

The letter

A bifolium: c. 16r in clear, c. 16v with ten clear lines, eight cipher lines, two clear lines and the date, c. 17r blank, c. 17v the address. There is no other cipher in it.

What it says

Italian as decoded, in the decode's own spelling; English from the translation in the repository (by Claude, not checked by a person or against any edition). * = word with a one-letter slip, counted unread; [?] = no sense or no key value.

The Duke has heard what "has newly happened in Genoa" and, given its importance, wants Ariosti to learn all he can about the causes of the rising and about how a person written in code (probably the Emperor, not confirmed) means to punish it. Then the reason:

"la quale essende* la scala de spagna in italla*, e da credere che [?] sia per fare ogni cosa per non la lassare andare in mano dei rancesi*""which, being the ladder of Spain into Italy, it is to be believed that [the person in code] will do everything not to let it fall into the hands of the French"

The cipher ends with an order to use extraordinary diligence to learn everything. The date fits the Fieschi rising at Genoa of early January 1547. from general knowledge, not checked in a source

The cipher

A sign-and-vowel table of the Este chancery, of the same family as the 1559 letter of the same writer and recipient but a different table. Raised figures give the vowel after a consonant (4 a, 5 e, 6 i, 7 o, 8 u). Some consonant signs take a raised figure; others take a slanting or upright stroke for the vowel. A bar over a sign doubles it, and dots on the line are nulls. One sign, used twice, stands for a person and has no value in the key.

How it was read

  1. 24 Nov 2026, before anything else. The prior-art search above, written down.
  2. Transcription. One Claude Fable 5.2 session copied the signs by shape from native-resolution crops, with no key (none existed). Twelve doubtful signs were marked. Lines 7-8 (60 of 276 signs) were set aside in advance as a held-out test. Frozen at 09:28 UTC.
  3. The key from lines 1-6 alone. A solver scored keys against an Italian language model. It fixed the vowel figures but not the consonants. A repeated pattern in the cipher, read as "intendere" and "intenderete", opened the consonants, and the rest followed by sense from lines 1-6. A caveat, stated in the record: "intendere" also stands in the clear lines of the same letter, which the solver could see.
  4. Frozen, then decoded once. The key was frozen at 09:54 UTC and all eight lines decoded in one pass. Nothing was emended: that pass is the figure of record.
  5. Judged by a separate session. A Claude Opus 5.5 session saw only the decode and scored each letter: sense, a one-letter slip, or no sense.

Checks

  • Held-out lines: 81 of the 92 letters of lines 7-8 decode to sense on the first pass (88.0%).
  • Shuffled keys: 0 of 5,000 shuffled keys score as well as the real key, at each of two seeds, on the held-out lines, the training lines and the whole page.
  • The repository's own statistical test: pass (the reading scores well above random keys and above a key fitted to shuffled ciphertext).
  • Second pass (28 Nov 2026): every transcription doubt and the named places were re-checked on the image, a v2 transcription frozen and decoded once with the same key: 5 changes (7 signs), 80 of 97 words (0.825). The model session that did this had seen the decode, so it was not blind. "del Flisco" is not supported by the image.
  • Brice Dudout's review (28 Nov 2026): reading review grade B. His own blind transcription of the page, frozen on 4 Nov 2026 before this work, agrees on 261 of 276 base signs (94.6%) and supports three of the second-pass changes (estraordinaria, circa, questo). Daniel Bourdeau merged the pull request the same day.

What remains uncertain

  • The end of line 4 and the start of line 5, about six words, do not read. A second reader on the image is needed.
  • The code sign for a person, twice. The Emperor fits; nothing confirms it.
  • Eleven one-letter slips are left as decoded. Emendations are proposed in the record but none is applied.
  • Weak key values: several signs rest on one or two uses.
  • The reading and the English have not been checked by a person or a reader of sixteenth-century Italian.
