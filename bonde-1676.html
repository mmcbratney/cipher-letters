Turn your arms on Denmark: Carl Bonde and Nils Lillieroot to Louis XIV, 6/16 October 1676

The Swedish envoys in Paris press Louis XIV to turn on Denmark, send help to Sweden and pay an extraordinary subsidy. With it, Bonde's Swedish despatch to Charles XI of 24 Nov/4 Dec 1677 in the same table. Read in part. The key was rebuilt from the Swedish chancery's decipherment of an earlier despatch, and finished from context.

What is new and what was known

No earlier reading or decipherment of the memorial or of the 1677 despatch was found, and no gloss is on these leaves. Priority is not established. The search (12 Nov 2026) covered LIBRIS, archive.org metadata and full text (32 phrase queries), the Google Books feed, Runeberg, Litteraturbanken, the Recueil des instructions for Sweden (1885), Varenius (1901), and Wimarson (1903, and Sveriges krig i Tyskland II, complete OCR). Not seen: Carl Trolle-Bonde's book on Carl Bonde, D.1 (Lund 1910), which is reported to print Bonde letters of 1676; Höjer 1875; Grauers 1926; and the French archives (Affaires étrangères, Correspondance politique, Suède 1676), where the memorial as handed to the French court may be in clear. Either could hold the text.

The key is not ours alone. Its base is the Swedish chancery's decipherment written in 1675 between the lines of Bonde's despatch of 11/21 Nov 1675 (image 102R). But about two fifths of its letter rows (52 of 119) were read from context on one page of the memorial (image 324), not from the gloss.

What this work adds: the memorial found and dated (its opening on f.306r, the enclosure to the despatch of 6/16 Oct 1676), transcriptions, a key rebuilt and tested on held-out pages, readings and English translations with holes marked, and a catalogue correction: images 107-109, listed as bare, are the cipher tail of the 1675 despatch and were already deciphered in the chancery gloss on images 102-106.

The letters

The memorial is in French, from the Swedish envoys in Paris, Carl Bonde and Nils Lillieroot, to Louis XIV. It follows the envoys' despatch of 6/16 Oct 1676 (image 321L) and is its enclosure. Long passages are in cipher; the rest is clear French.

The despatch of 24 Nov/4 Dec 1677 is in Swedish, from Bonde to Charles XI. Its only cipher is on image 489R: 17 runs, 169 numbers inside clear sentences, no gloss. It was filed separately and folded into this entry because it uses the same number table.

What the letters say

French and Swedish as read; English from the translations in the repository. [314: Danemarc] = code 314, valued from the 1675 gloss but counted not read; [435] = a number with no value; [?word] = a sense from context, not read; [.] = a sign with no value; a letter in brackets = supplied.

The memorial, 6/16 October 1676

The envoys thank Louis XIV for his declaration of war on Denmark under the guarantee of the Northern treaties, and for Pomponne's assurance that France will not abandon Charles XI. They report the victory near Halmstad and the raising of the siege of Bohus. In cipher and clear together they press him: the allies, having lost hope of retaking what France has won, will throw their weight on Sweden.

"Il n'en faut pas douter pourveu que Vostre Majesté trouve bon de tourner ses armes contre le ro[y] de [314: Danemarc] et d'env[o][y]er de bonne heure au secours d[e] la Suede le nombre de troupp[es] et de vaisseau[x] qui soit prop[or]tionné à la grandeur [du] dan[g]er""There need be no doubt of it, provided Your Majesty sees fit to turn his arms against the King of [314: Denmark] and to send early to the help of Sweden the number of troops and ships in proportion to the size of the danger"

Pomponne has told them that is impossible:

"l'impossibilité qu'ele trouve à [.] faire la guerre [a]u roy de [314: Danemarc] et à donner le secours [à] l[a] Suede""the impossibility he finds in making war on the King of [314: Denmark] and in giving help to Sweden"

So let him ready a squadron for the start of spring 1677 to reach Gothenburg and join the ten ships there, and keep it secret (this passage is on image 324, which is in-sample for the key). The allies will make no peace until France returns its conquests, count on taking Pomerania, and court Bavaria, Hanover, Denmark and Holland. If France makes peace, it should be on a condition that puts Charles XI back in possession of his places. Charles XI cannot meet the cost from his ruined provinces and the ordinary subsidy: let France grant an extraordinary sum, part to buy provisions and munitions in England and Holland for Stralsund and Gothenburg, part for Pomerania, and win Hamburg to the cause with freedom of trade.

The despatch, 24 November/4 December 1677

The French court is "very ill pleased": on reaching Livonia and learning of "Pac's opposition", someone would "delay and hold back" a march (the clear lines around it are not transcribed). The court wants a prompt diversion:

"intet högre åstundar, än att the desseinerne med en prompt diuersion uti [435] med thet fordeligaste executerade""wants nothing more than that the designs, with a prompt diversion into [435] [?Prussia], be carried out to the best advantage"

The cipher

Homophonic substitution on numbers, one number a letter. In the memorial's key, seven numbers each stand for e, f and n, and six for l and o. A few numbers stand for ss, st, ff and ll. Word codes run from about 240 (314 = Danmarck in the 1675 gloss). The signs 173-178 and the single digits 3, 4 and 8 stand alone between words and look like nulls; nothing proves it. Numbers are separated by dots, with no word division.

The same table serves Bonde's Swedish despatches of 1675-1678 in the volume. So 66 is å in Swedish and is written for y in this French text, and 89 is n in the 1675 gloss and l here.

How it was read

  1. 12 Nov 2026. Key v1 was rebuilt from the chancery's decipherment on image 102R by aligning 488 cipher numbers with 566 gloss letters.
  2. Control: ten rows of the bare image 107L were transcribed, frozen, decoded once with v1, and then found word for word in the gloss on image 105L. So images 107-109 were already deciphered in 1675.
  3. Key v2 added values corrected from 107L/105L and values for numbers 100-176 fixed on 14 training lines of image 324.
  4. First test, failed. The other 30 lines of image 324 were frozen and decoded once with v2: 44 of 103 words (42.72%), under the 65% bar set beforehand.
  5. Key v3 (md5 8e1d93d1188e698ad6dd14826e8ba168, frozen 02:55:22): v2 plus 23 values read off the image-324 test lines after that test. So image 324 is in-sample for v3 and is not counted. 128 rows.
  6. Nine other pages were transcribed after v3 was frozen, each frozen before a single decode with v3 unchanged. No value was changed after those decodes.
  7. The 1677 despatch: key v9, a later state of the same table (v3 corrected at 66, 89, 21, 103, 131, 176 and 335 and extended by 36 rows), frozen at 06:08:58 UTC by the contributor's record. The transcription of 489R was frozen at 06:24:26 and decoded once: 24 of 30 words.

Checks

  • Shuffled keys: 5,000 per test, two seeds, French 4-gram model: 0 of 5,000 on every memorial page. All nine pages pooled: true score -2.1534 against best shuffles -4.6480 and -4.5698. The 1677 despatch, Swedish model: 0 of 5,000 (true -3.1451 against -4.0151 and -3.9637), a weak test of 30 words.
  • Blind second transcriptions: f.307v and f.309v, by a separate session: 860 of 863 full numerals the same. 489R: 168 of 169 numbers the same; its decode gives 23 of 30 words (76.67%).
  • Clear text: in the 1677 despatch the clear Swedish between the runs agrees with the decode, which is the stronger evidence for that short text.
  • Integration check (by an AI agent for the maintainer, not a person): a decoder written from the key alone decodes all 284 cipher lines of the nine held-out pages and of 489R identically. About 400 groups were compared with the Riksarkivet images (489R, all 17 runs; f.307r lines 1-6 and 18-22; f.309v lines 4-12): 0 disagreements.
  • Time stamp: an RFC 3161 stamp of 02:54:07 GMT, 12 Nov 2026, covers a file byte-identical to key v3, before any stamp that holds a held-out page. Everything else about the order of events is by the contributor's record.

A unit is a word of the plaintext, read only if every sign gives its frozen value and the word is spelt. Codes, numerals cut at the gutter, unvalued and weak values and emended words never count. On the nine held-out pages 94.7% of cipher numbers (3,407 of 3,599) carry a letter value, of which 56.5% come from the gloss and 43.5% from context. In the despatch, 58 of the 149 numbers inside the read words come from context, and 22 of the 24 read words use at least one context value.

Ten more Bonde despatches of 1676-1678 were decoded with later states of the key and are in the folder, each counted on its own. One, 12/22 Aug 1676, reads at 494 of 705 (70.07%), but Wimarson (1903) already paraphrases its substance, except the Feuquières passage. The other nine are under the contributor's 65% line: decoded, not read.

What remains uncertain

  • Word codes 240, 245, 267, 324, 463 and 314 are not read (senses from context only). 314 = Danmarck by the 1675 gloss, but counted not read by rule.
  • Seventeen numbers have no value in v3. Values proposed after the decodes are never counted; testing them needs fresh text in this table, and the volume's bare French cipher is used up.
  • 68 words hold a numeral cut at the gutter, lost in the binding.
  • The probable nulls (173-178, 3, 4, 8) are not proved.
  • The first filing counted 12 words as read whose 66 gives å where French needs y. They are downgraded: the figure is 71.47%, not the 73.66% first filed.
  • Image 324 is in-sample for the key, and its reading does not bracket its supplied letters.
  • The 1677 despatch is 30 words, too short to say much about the key. Its codes 418 (twice) and 435 are not read.
  • Trolle-Bonde D.1, Höjer, Grauers and the French archives were not seen, so priority is not established.

Who did what

The key's base is the Swedish chancery's interlinear decipherment of Bonde's despatch of 11/21 Nov 1675; later chancery glosses of 1676-1677 gave values of key v9. Malcolm McBratney (Brisbane) contributed the work, with Claude (Anthropic) sessions surveying the volume, transcribing, rebuilding the key, reading, translating and checking. The integration checks were made by an AI agent for the maintainer, Daniel Bourdeau; the memorial and the 1677 despatch were joined as one entry at integration, and the further despatches were filed here at his request. Brice Dudout (issue #629, with AI agents) identified images 113 right and 114 left as one letter of November 1675 in a second code, deciphered by its period gloss; that letter is not part of this key.

Claude sessions did the transcriptions, the key, the reading and the translations; the second transcriptions and the image checks at integration were also made by AI agents. No person has compared any transcription with the images.
