"The thing will not succeed": Girolamo Muzio to Ferrante Gonzaga, 11 January 1550

A page of cipher written from Rome during the conclave of 1549-50, on a courier said to bring the Emperor's consent to Monte as Pope. Read in part, with a key started from an early hand's decipherment on the sister letter of the next day.

What is new and what was known

Part of this was known. On the sister letter of 12 January an early hand, not Muzio's, had already written letters over one cipher line (seven signs) and a decipherment of the c. 5v passage in the margin. The date of that hand is not established. That early work gave the first values of the key.

No reading of the c. 3r cipher block was found. Ronchini's Lettere di Girolamo Muzio (Parma 1864) has a different, clear letter of 11 Jan 1550 (no. LXXVI) and none of 12 Jan; it prints neither cipher text. Also checked on 11 Nov 2026: the Lettere of 1590, Pastor vol. 13, Vandini's appendices to the Campori catalogue, the Estense records, the Cyphersolver repository, Cryptiana and HistoCrypt. Not checked: the Parma originals of Ferrante Gonzaga's papers, where a key or a deciphered copy may be; Negri's 2000 edition of the Lettere; Concilium Tridentinum vol. II; studies of the conclave.

What this work adds: the key completed, a reading of 0.909 of the c. 3r block, a test of the key against the early marginal decipherment, two code values, and an English translation.

The letters

c. 3r carries one block of cipher, 25 lines, with a clear heading and four clear closing lines. All of the news is in cipher. c. 3v and c. 4r are blank; c. 4v has the address and docket. The catalogue's "cc. 3-4, Lettera cifrata" is one page of cipher.

The sister letter of 12 January (cc. 5r-5v) is mostly clear, with two cipher passages: 9 lines on c. 5r and 3 lines on c. 5v, 339 units in all.

The cipher mentions "una altra mia, che vien con questa" on a note of the Cardinal de' Medici. That companion letter is in print: Ronchini no. LXXV, 10 Jan 1550, pp. 132-133.

What Muzio writes

Italian as read; English from the translation in the repository. [?word] = value from context only; [13], [24] = codes with no value; [Cardinale], [V. Ecc.] = codes confirmed by the c. 5v margin; fo[ss]e = emended.

A courier has come back from the imperial court. He was sent to ask the Emperor (code 26, our proposal) to accept Santa Croce or Monte as Pope, a scheme helped by the Duke of Florence, and the answer seems to be that the Emperor accepts Monte. Muzio's source is "the Prior of [24]". He thinks the news true but does not expect the scheme to succeed.

"Io, considerata la autorità del [Cardinale] nostro, la congiuntion sua con Ferrara, la aut[o]rità di Ferrara co°[?n gli] Francesi, mi persuado che la cosa non habia a riuscire, salvo se da tutto° il mondo non siamo abandonati.""For my part, considering the authority of our [Cardinal], his bond with Ferrara, and the authority of Ferrara with [?the] French, I am persuaded that the thing will not succeed, unless we are abandoned by the whole world."

Nor does he see how the Imperialists, who have no liking for Monte, would give him their votes, knowing this is not the Emperor's true wish.

The cipher of the sister letter reports talk from the conclave: Ferrante and Don Diego had backed Salviati; the Dukes of Florence and Alba disclosed it to the Emperor; Salviati was excluded and Don Diego drew back.

Del Monte was elected Julius III on 7 Feb 1550. The identifications of persons (the Emperor for 26, Ercole Gonzaga for "our Cardinal", Ippolito d'Este for "Ferrara", Diego Hurtado de Mendoza for Don Diego) are ours, from context. unverified: not checked against a study of the conclave

The cipher

A simple substitution on letter-like signs and single digits, written without word division. There are two signs each for a, e, i, o, u and s, and own signs for the doubled letters ll, rr, tt, pp, nn and ff. Four digit pairs (38, 39, 42, 43) are nulls, and a barred circle marks a stop between clauses. A small code has the number groups 13, 24, 25, 26, 1o and 4o and one sign, CE, for titles and persons. The key has 47 rows.

The o sign and the i sign differ by one minim: three or two minims, then a tail. All but one of the differences between the two transcriptions are there.

How it was read

  1. 10 Nov 2026. First transcription of c. 3r (904 tokens, 29 types). The sign set was compared with the Gonzaga keys in the repository (gonzaga1549, WAINUT's table; ercole1544; capilupi1545; bresegno1548): none fits.
  2. 11 Nov. An annealing solver with an Italian four-letter model failed on the letter (best -4.30 per character, no Italian). The first transcription had merged look-alike signs.
  3. 11 Nov. On the sister letter, an early hand had written t r a d ij o a over one cipher line. With the two signs before them this gave c o n t r a d. The clear text around the passage and the word dividers on the sheet then gave "per questo" and "scoperta questa pratica", and so most of the alphabet.
  4. 11 Nov. c. 3r transcribed again with the look-alike signs split (861 units), and the key completed by reading. The reader knew values from line 5 on.
  5. 12 Nov. The c. 5v passage, not used before, was decrypted with the key as it stood, and compared with the early hand's margin beside it (below). This gave two code values: CE = Cardinale, 25 = V. Ecc.
Cipher, by the key: "scrivere al CE nostro ma poi non havendo risposta di quello che 25 ha mandato". Margin: "scriver al Car.le n[ost]ro, ma poi no[n] havendo risposta di q[ue]llo ch[e] V. Ex. gli ha ma[ndato]""write to our [Cardinal]; but then, having no answer to what [Your Excellency] has sent"

Checks

  • The early margin: the c. 5v decryption matches the marginal decipherment word for word, except for a "gli" the margin adds. The key had not seen that passage.
  • Blind second transcription: another session, with no key and no reading, agrees with the first on 851 of 861 units (98.84%). The 10 differences were looked at on the image at full resolution: the blind reader was right on 9, the first reader on 1.
  • Shuffled keys (on the first version of the transcription): the key's score on c. 3r was -3.922 per character against a best of -6.295 for 5,000 shuffled keys, none as good. Lines 21-25 with a key voted from lines 1-20 only: -3.866 against -5.965. The c. 5r passage with the c. 3r key: -4.012 against -5.951.
  • Statistical validation: the repository's test on the second version gives a pass (reading -1.882 per letter, random keys -4.679, a key annealed onto shuffled ciphertext at best -3.187).
  • Maintainer's check: Daniel Bourdeau's own decoder gave the same text on issue 405, and he wrote "the reading holds". He pointed to "difficile", "ne so come" (line 20) and "i voti" (line 23); all three were confirmed on the image. At integration about 70 signs of lines 5-7 were compared with the image and agree; the verification of the issue had compared about 150 signs.

What remains uncertain

  • Code groups 13 and 24 have no value. 26 (Sua Maestà), 4o (Duca di) and 1o (Don Diego) rest on context.
  • The single signs PSI and OP on c. 3r and OX on c. 5r each occur once.
  • The omega sign reads ss three times (fosse, creasse, esser): a polyphone, or a second form not told apart on the image.
  • The sister letter's cipher was read once, at 60-70% scale, by a reader who knew the key; one sign is unread and three words are doubtful. Its clear text is not collated.
  • The early hand on cc. 5r-5v is not dated; only the archive can date it.
  • The Parma originals of Ferrante Gonzaga's papers were not reached.

Who did what

The first values of the key come from an unnamed early hand that deciphered seven signs and one passage of the sister letter. Malcolm McBratney (Brisbane) contributed the work and sent it as issue #405 on 11 Nov 2026, with Claude (Anthropic) models doing the transcriptions, solving, scripts and checks. Daniel Bourdeau checked the issue on 12 Nov 2026 and integrated the work in Cyphersolver (issue #405, PR #445). The Rossi letters of the same catalogue entry are in a different cipher: Rossi to Ferrante Gonzaga, 1537.

Several sessions of one model family did the transcriptions and the reading. No person read the signs without AI. The first reader of c. 3r was not blind from line 5 on; the independence here is the blind second transcription and the c. 5v margin. Daniel Bourdeau's decoder reproduced the text, and image comparisons were made at the check and at integration.
