Flowers of sulphur and the corruption of Sol: Johann Gerlach's cipher recipe book, 1572-76

A German alchemist's notebook written in pictorial signs. The key is Julius Kohn's, made in 1889 and filed in the volume. What is new here is the reading of seventeen pages, for which no published reading was found.

What is new and what was known

The cipher was broken long ago. Julius Kohn wrote a key into the volume in 1889, and the Wellcome catalogue says "Kohn's key inserted". The key is two leaves of sign groups in rows, dated 1889, with no letters written beside them.

No published reading of the text was found in online searches (10-11 Nov 2026). German specialist literature, Kohn's papers and the Wellcome files were not checked. So this is a reading with an existing key, not a first break.

What this work adds: a sign-by-sign transcription of seventeen pages, a decode with Kohn's key set up as a table, a reading in Early New High German and an English translation. The rest of the cipher (pp.20-178) is not read, and stays in the Cyphersolver catalogue as entry 1252, narrowed.

The book

The manuscript is a notebook of recipes, not a letter. Its cipher pages hold alchemical and medical recipes. The pages read here run from medicines made of sulphur and antimony to the preparation of gold.

Pages 16-19 are a numbered sequence with a heading. The margin, in ordinary script, sends the reader on to later pages: "1. sal armoniack fol. 124, 2. spiritus vini fol. 125, 3. aqua regis fol. 137". Pages 11-12 are a narrow inserted leaf with no scan of its own.

What Gerlach writes

German as read; English from the translation in the repository. ? after a letter = doubtful sign; [..] = supplied, restored or emended; [...] = not read. The recipes are given as 16th-century evidence only.

Flowers of sulphur (pp.7-10)

Sulphur is washed in vinegar, dried, then sublimed three times with colcothar and salt. The result is called "the very noblest medicine for the lungs and the whole chest".

"Nemet schonen gelben schwefel, souil ir wolet, stoset in klein zu pulfer""Take fine yellow sulphur, as much as you wish, pound it small to powder"

Mixed with myrrh, asa dulcis and sugar candy and taken in a soft egg morning and evening, the powder is said to cure a cough:

"sollet [stillet?] allen husten und heil?et die lunge, weret der schwindtsucht, m?er? dan man daruon sagen thar.""[stills?] every cough and heals the lungs, wards off consumption, more than one dares say of it."

Antimony and gold (pp.13-19)

Red and white flowers of antimony, and a fever remedy taken in wine before bed (pp.13-14). Then "how one shall make quicksilver out of gold" (p.15), and the numbered preparations for the corruption of Sol: sal armoniack sublimed six times through sal gemme, spirit of wine from burnt wine with calcined tartar, and an aqua regis of vitriol, saltpetre and sal armoniack.

"Was zu der coruption solis gehorig ist.""What belongs to the corruption of Sol (coruption solis)."

Openings of later sections

Only the first lines are read of pp.40 (the practice of the philosophers' stone), 107 and 112 (an oak chest for putrefaction), 115 (mercurius solis et lunae), 156 (the arcanum tartari) and 177 (oils and herb juices).

The cipher

A simple homophonic substitution of German. The contributor's table has 52 sign labels: one to three signs per letter, once four. There are no nulls and no code groups. A comma-like sign divides words. Alchemical pictograms (antimony, Mars, Mercury, Sol) stand for themselves. The Wellcome catalogue says 51 symbols; the two counts are not reconciled.

Kohn's sheet runs from Z to A in groups of signs, with no letters. The contributor gave each group its letter by that order. He says Kohn's u, t and s groups are one step out of line with the text values, and moved them. Three figure-4 shapes stand for l (open), r (closed, with a point) and u (closed, modern), and were assigned partly from context. So Kohn's key is applied with that alignment, not unchanged.

How it was read

  1. 28-29 Oct 2026. Daniel Bourdeau listed the manuscript as Cyphersolver catalogue entry 1252, in a sweep of UK libraries, noting Kohn's key.
  2. 10 Nov 2026. Kohn's key set up as a table of 52 sign labels. Pages 7-10, 13-19 and parts of six later pages transcribed sign by sign from the Wellcome images: 348 lines, 6,479 lettered signs, 143 marked doubtful.
  3. Each page decoded with the table and built into sentences, then translated. Other model sessions made the first passes of pp.16-18 and independent passes of pp.8-10 and 13.
  4. 11 Nov 2026. A blind control on p.8: transcribed and decoded once with the key frozen, by the contributor's record. Sent as GitHub issue #439.

Checks

  • Decoder re-run: at integration (12 Nov 2026) a decoder written afresh from the key table reproduced all 348 decoded lines from the transcription.
  • Images: Kohn's two leaves and the first six lines of p.8 (130 signs) were compared with the Wellcome images and agree. The other pages were not compared at that check.
  • Brice Dudout's verification (14 Nov 2026, PR #570): his own decoder gave 348 of 348 lines. He re-read p.7 on the full-size image: all 45 doubtful figure-4 signs agree with their shapes, and three signs change (souil, wolet, ruret). Doubtful signs on p.7 went from 54 to 7. Lines L07, L08, L12 and L13 (about 75 signs) were then checked on the same image and agree.
  • Not re-run: the contributor's word-list test (77% to 96% of decoded words found in a 16th-century word list per page; 1,000 shuffled keys per page at most 30%) and his agreement figures between transcribers. The lists and scripts were not sent.

The p.7 figure rests on the marks of the reading only. Five more words of that page stand on a sign still marked doubtful in the transcription; with them p.7 would be 87 of 96 (90.6%). The contributor's own figures count signs, not words (for example p.13 515 of 536 signs, 96.1%), and are higher.

Two later tests of the key, on pp.124-137 and pp.138, 139, 140 and 150 (16 Nov 2026), were decoded blind and judged letter by letter: 5,667 of 6,097 letters (92.9%) and 1,573 of 1,652 (95.2%). Both passed a shuffled-key control (0 of 5,000 at two seeds). The repository's reading check rates both "decoded, not read", so they are not counted above.

What remains uncertain

  • 96 signs remain doubtful, mostly the three figure-4 shapes and the s-signs.
  • p.9 lines 21-25 and two lines of p.10 are stained or damaged in the scan.
  • Pages 11-12, the inserted leaf, have no scan.
  • Pages 16-18 rest on single readers from other model families (p.18 two, adjudicated) and are not rated.
  • Signs on Kohn's sheet not yet seen in the text (for e, x, y and z) are untested.
  • The rest of the cipher, pp.20-178, is not read, apart from the opening lines above and the two blind tests.
