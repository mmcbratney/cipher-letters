No war on Sweden without a fleet: Conrad Biermann to Paul von Klingenberg, February to July 1675

The Danish secretary's letters to the envoy at The Hague in spring 1675: the fleet, the regiments and boatmen, the Elector of Brandenburg. A cipher of letter signs, syllables and word codes, read in part with the key recovered from the ciphertext alone.

Read in part: 3,648 of 4,709 tokens (77.5%), eleven letters Key recovered from the ciphertext alone AI-assisted; no person has read the letters without AI

What is new and what was known

No key sheet and no decipherment of these letters was found. The repository's own survey of the copybook (targets/biermann1675) listed the Klingenberg letters as "key unknown". Brice Dudout's L'Europe chiffrée names Klingenberg only. Klingenberg's own papers in the Rigsarkivet (guide nos. 278 and 327), where the letters he received may stand deciphered, were not checked. Priority is not established.

The printed Privy Council protocol of 1675 (Aarsberetninger fra det Kongelige Geheimearchiv VI, pp. 117-126) and Historisk Tidsskrift 5. R. IV pp. 446-450 print the policy the letters carry, but no letter to Klingenberg and no cipher. For 13 of the 22 further drafts the content is already in print in summary; the deciphered wording is not.

A separate key. The same copybook holds letters to Høeg and Meyercron that the repository and Brice Dudout read with a key sheet on image 55 (K55). That work is theirs. Nothing here uses K55, and nothing here claims those letters. The Klingenberg letters use a different key, rebuilt here.

What this work adds: the Klingenberg key rebuilt from the letters themselves, eleven letters decoded with maps frozen beforehand, German readings with every hole in place and English translations. Catalogue entry 465 was narrowed to the letters still unread.

The letters

Conrad Biermann was the secretary whose copybook records outgoing letters to Danish ministers abroad. His letters to Klingenberg at The Hague run from 6 February to 24 June 1675 in the copybook, with three more as drafts in Biermann's own hand in the unit that follows. Clear German and cipher alternate inside the sentence.

The letters belong to the Scanian War in spring 1675. They carry the King's reply on the fleet, the regiments and boatmen, and the Elector of Brandenburg. from the repository's summary; not checked against a history

What Biermann writes

German as read; English from the repository's translation (by Claude, not checked by a German-reading person). Italics = from cipher; plain = the clear text of the copybook, read approximately.

4 May: the fleet

The letter opens with the King's surprise at the allies' view that he is bound to break with Sweden; the words on subsidies for his fleet are partly holes. Biermann answers that war on Sweden needs a fleet:

"schweden zu wasser der grosseste abbruch geschehen muß, so aber ohne flotte nicht zu practicieren stehet""the greatest harm to Sweden by water must be done, which however cannot be practised without a fleet"

Without one, Sweden would have "as it were a free hand". The next passage, with several holes, turns to the men the treaties stipulate.

1 June: boatmen and the Elector

Biermann is ordered to send Klingenberg the list of regiments he asked for, and to report that the King agrees to his proposal on boatmen:

"Ihr Königl. Mayt. allergnädigst zufrieden daß die bedeutete drey tausend botsleute""His Royal Majesty is most graciously content that the said three thousand boatmen"
"wegen des Herrn Envoyé extraord. Höeg sendung an churfürst""concerning the Envoy Extraordinary Høeg's mission to the Elector"

No final resolution has been taken on that mission, but Biermann expects it to go ahead.

The cipher

  1. Letter signs, one per letter: 29 signs. In the copybook hand c is a large double-barred sign and g a small single-barred one.
  2. A syllable table, 432 to 525, in alphabetical order: a vowel row, then rows of five syllables for each consonant (437 ba ... 525 zu). The d row has four.
  3. Numbers 1 to 430 for words, names and frequent word parts, in no order found. The final map (v4) gives 106 of them a value.

A German key sheet endorsed 1675 on images 94/95 is not this key as it stands: its values differ from those found here, and its blank slots are the groups that read here as die, in, ver, und and nicht. A sibling relation is not excluded.

How it was read

  1. The letters were chosen from the repository's survey of the copybook. The signs came from a probable word and repeats, the syllable table from its alphabetical structure, and the word codes from repeated contexts in the letters themselves. Sixteen letters, 6 February to 21 June, served to build the key.
  2. The word map grew in four versions: v1 (50 values), v2 (54), v3 (78), v4 (106). Each was frozen before the letters it was tested on (by the contributor's record); v3 and v4 are time-stamped before the letters of the count.
  3. The test. The 4 May letter (the letter of issue #424) and seven later copybook letters were decoded once with v3; the three drafts once with v4. None had been decoded before its map was frozen (by the contributor's record).
  4. 12 Nov 2026. Two numerals of the 4 May transcription were corrected after the review, 441 to 447 (certain) and 511 to 501 (probable). The count of record uses the frozen text.
  5. 13 Nov 2026. 22 further passages from the drafts units, transcribed and decoded once with v4 on 12 Nov, were added (PR #490), with German readings and English translations (PR #496). They are not in the count of record.

Checks

  • Reproduction (integration, 12 Nov 2026): the counting script reproduces every line of the counts. An independent decoder written from the key alone gives the 4 May decryption token for token (729 of 729).
  • Time stamps: three Sectigo tokens carry their manifest hashes. Map v3 is in the first stamp, before the seven letters appear; map v4 is stamped before the three drafts' transcriptions. When the transcriptions were made rests on the contributor's record.
  • Images: the project's verifier (a model check, not a person) compared 333 groups of the 4 May letter (331 agree; the other two are the corrected numerals), 62 groups of 1 June and 63 of the 28 June draft (all agree). The other letters were not compared.
  • Controls: shuffled signs and syllables, 0 of 5,000 (z 7.6). The word-code layer is only weakly separated by a character model (z 3.1), so the word values rest on context and on exact decoding of later letters.
  • Statistical validation: the repository's test gives "pass", with a partial German corpus in that run.

Limits. The intended words were written after decoding by the same model that built the key, and the transcriber knew the key. There is no blind second transcription of these eleven. Counting only words built from signs and syllables, with no word code, gives 1,334 of 4,709 (28.3%). The 77.47% is the headline; the 28.3% is the floor.

Further passages, not in the count. 22 passages of the drafts: the first reader 6,103 of 8,587 tokens (71.07%) and 2,561 of 3,386 word units (75.63%); blind second readers 2,555 of 3,260 word units (78.37%). No passage reaches 95%. Ten more passages were parked by the first reader and are not shown. On 16 Nov 2026 two blind decodes were added as "decoded, not read": the letter body 134R-136L (88.47% of units) and the opening pages 41R-43R of a draft (95.04% of decode units, 88.8% of words). A blind attempt on 253R-254L failed (59.2%).

What remains uncertain

  • Word codes with no value: 54 tokens of the 4 May letter under v3, 25 under v4. More letters or Klingenberg's own deciphered letterbook would fix them.
  • In the three drafts the reader wrote every hash-like sign as the c sign. 96 failed tokens differ from the intended word only by c where g is wanted. A blind re-reading with a ledger is still owed.
  • The sixteen letters used to build the key have no reading and no count.
  • The clear text between the cipher passages is read approximately.
  • The intended words and the English have not been checked by a German reader.
  • Klingenberg's legation papers were not checked, so priority is not established.

Who did what

Malcolm McBratney (Brisbane) directed the work and set its rules, with Claude (Anthropic) sessions choosing the letters from the repository's survey, transcribing, rebuilding the key, reading and checking. He sent it as issue #424 and PR #455, with further passages in PR #490 and PR #496.

Daniel Bourdeau's survey of the copybook and its image list (targets/biermann1675) are where the letters were chosen from. His review on issue #424 found the two misread numerals of the 4 May letter, the true count of 54 open-code tokens (the issue had said 42) and the four-syllable d row (the issue had said five). The K55 key and the Høeg and Meyercron letters are the work of the repository and of Brice Dudout.

Claude sessions made the transcriptions, the key, the intended words, the readings and the English. The image comparisons in the integration checks were made by a model check, not a person. No person has read the letters without AI, and no German reader has checked the readings.
