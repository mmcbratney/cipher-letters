"Wolfe Tone before the Directory": Fenian letter No. II, London, 24 June 1885

An unsigned letter in English reports a "most complete success": an interview with a man written only as a number code, at which the writer spoke for Irish nationalists. The names and key words are in two-digit numbers. The key was recovered here from the ciphertext. No key or printed reading was found in the places searched.

What is new and what was known

The DECODE database, Cyphersolver, JSTOR and the web were searched: no key, decipherment or printed reading of this letter was found. A Fenian list of code words in the same collection is an open word code for cables, with no table of numbers for letters: not this key.

Not checked: the books most likely to print or discuss the letter (Devoy's Post Bag, 1948-53; K. R. M. Short, The Dynamite War, 1979; Niall Whelehan, The Dynamiters, 2012; Shane Kenna, War in the Shadows, 2014; work on F. F. Millen), because no full text could be reached. A historian may well have read so simple an alphabet in print without calling it a decipherment. Not finding a reading is not proof that none exists.

What this work adds, as far as checked: the key, a frozen decode of every group, and a reading with every hole marked.

The letter

The writer reached home at 7.30 pm, had the friend's letter at 9, and writes "to allay your anxiety". The sheet is headed "Letter No. II" and breaks off at the foot of side 4 in the middle of a quotation; the rest, and letter No. I, are not in this item.

What it says

The letter's own English; cipher words in ⟦ ⟧; letters in [ ] are supplied where the writer abbreviated, and are not in the cipher.

With a go-between called "the Infant", the writer met ⟦DEC⟧ in a richly furnished apartment. The Infant's written digest set out "the mutual advantages likely to accrue to ⟦Rus[si]a⟧, and ourselves". Questioned in English, the writer felt himself "⟦Wolfe Tone⟧ before the Directory". He said:

"we were not ⟦R[...]s⟧ in the strict sense of the word but ⟦Pat[riot]s⟧ struggling for the ⟦Aut[ono]my⟧ of ⟦our⟧ ⟦nat[iv]e⟧ ⟦l[an]d⟧"The writer's own words. "R—s" is open: revolutionaries, rebels and republicans all fit.

He also said they were not alone a ⟦secret⟧ ⟦or[ganisation]⟧ but had "the whole ⟦Irish World⟧ with us". The sheet ends with ⟦DEC⟧ asking: "do you think a diversion in ⟦C[a]nada⟧ feasible". The setting (1885, the Afghan crisis between Britain and Russia, Irish-American approaches to Russia) is our inference. not checked in the literature

The cipher

Each letter is a two-digit number, from one alphabet shifted along the numbers 60 to 85. The writer runs the digits together and often writes only the first letters and the last letter of a word, with a dash (RUS-A, PAT--S, L-D). The code hides names and key words in an otherwise clear letter.

How it was read

  1. Prior art first, as above.
  2. Transcription. One Claude Opus 5.5 session read the letter in the JSTOR viewer and copied the 13 number groups. Frozen at 23:31 UTC on 25 Nov 2026. Disclosed in the record: the transcriber had seen the pattern that gave the key before the freeze, and used it to look again at two doubtful pairs. So the transcription is not blind to the key, and there is no held-out part.
  3. The key. "85 68 85 69 84 / 73 65 68 62 80" follows "we had the whole … with us". The repeat at the start fits IRI-, and IRISH WORLD fits all ten pairs under one shift. One shift fixes all 26 values.
  4. Checked on words not used to build it: WOLFE TONE, CANADA, RUS-A, PAT--S, OUR NAT-E L-D.

Checks

  • Other keys: five test words, fixed in advance (IRISH, WORLD, WOLFE, TONE, OUR): the real key reads 5 of 5; the other 25 shifts read none; 5,000 random keys and 5,000 shuffles of the ciphertext, at each of two seeds, never reach the real key (best 2 of 5). WOLFE, TONE and OUR are independent of the crib.
  • Blind second reading: an isolated AI reader, given the viewer crops only, agrees on 70 of 71 pairs. It also sees one extra pair, 64 (N), after OR——, which would fit the writer's habit for "organisation"; not counted.
  • The repository's own statistical test: too short to decide (under 80 letters).
  • Not yet done: a person comparing the numbers with the image (the PDF has been downloaded for this), and the maintainers' review.

The eight holes are DEC four times (the letters are read; the person is not identified), OR—— (organisation or order), R——s, Aut[ono]my (the writer wrote AUTNONMY) and C[a]nada (one pair doubtful). Read in part.

What remains uncertain

  • Who DEC is. The same three numbers in all four places; code name, initials or abbreviation, not known.
  • R——s: only R and S are written.
  • One doubtful pair in CANADA: the digits are clipped at the page edge in the viewer.
  • The rest of the series: letter No. I and the continuation were not found in the collection.
  • The books listed above, which may already print the letter.
