Pepper, Japan and the gold and silver islands: the VOC's number cipher, Batavia 1623-1643

Letters from the Dutch East India Company's Governor-General and Council at Batavia to its directors, the Heren XVII, with their sensitive passages in a number cipher. One key, rebuilt from the clerks' own glosses on a letter of 1634, decodes letters of 1623, 1640 and 1643. Parts were known before: the 1623 passages were deciphered at the time, and P. A. Leupe printed the 1643 passage and its meaning in 1858.

What is new and what was known

1623: not first. The 1623 passages were deciphered at the time: the explanation is written in the margins of the copy in inv. 1079, and W. Ph. Coolhaas noted it in his edition of the Generale Missiven (vol. I, 1960). Our decode agrees with those margins word for word, except one word.

1643: not first. P. A. Leupe printed the cipher passage of the 23 January 1643 letter, numbers and all, with the meaning of its cipher words, in his edition of the voyage of Maarten Gerritsz Vries (Reize van Maarten Gerritsz. Vries in 1643, Amsterdam, 1858). We found this only after our reading. His numbers agree with ours, 18 of 18, checked on the page image. Credit for that passage is his.

Not checked: whether Cheng Shaogang's dissertation (1995, vol. II p. 27) prints the 1623 passage; HathiTrust and Google Books full text. VOC ciphers are known as a class: the DECODE database and a HistoCrypt paper by Dinnissen and Kopal (2021) describe a different VOC cipher of 1674.

What this work adds, as far as checked: the number table itself, rebuilt from the 1634 glosses and shown to read across twenty years; frozen tests of it on the 1640 and 1643 letters before their glosses were opened; and the second letter system, B, from 1640 on. No printed number table for these years was found.

The letters

The Company's general letters from Batavia went home in several copies by different ships. Some copies carry the clerks' glosses over the cipher; others are bare. That is what made the work possible: the glossed 1634 letter gave the key, and the bare copies of 1640 and 1643 could be tested with it before their glossed twins were looked at.

What they say

Dutch normalised from the decode; English by Claude, not checked by a person or a reader of seventeenth-century Dutch.

1623: the yacht de Hase is to sail for the Netherlands four days after the English ships, and the cargo is listed:

"In 't Wapen en Leyden sijn geladen acht en twintich duysent sacken peper, acht hondert sockels folie, twee hondert tachtich duysent ponden noten moschaten""In the Wapen and the Leyden are loaded twenty-eight thousand sacks of pepper, eight hundred bales of mace, two hundred and eighty thousand pounds of nutmegs"

1643: great hopes of the Japan trade, the Chinese and other nations shut out, and the plan for a voyage of discovery:

"Tot ontdeckinge vande Noordt Custe [Tartarien] ende ... de XXI ende IV rijcke [eylanden] bij [oosten] [Japan]""To discover the north coast of Tartary, and ... the silver- and gold-rich islands east of Japan"

That is the voyage of Maarten Gerritsz Vries of 1643, with the flute Castricum and the yacht Breskens. The words in brackets are system B words, read from the glosses, not from a tested key.

The cipher

System A is a homophonic number cipher: each common letter has two to four numbers, and a stroke divides the words in the copies of 1623 to 1640. In the 1643 bare copy the clerk wrote a stroke between every number, so the word division was lost; a rule for finding the words was frozen before that letter was decoded. System B writes each plain letter as another letter or a number (r = a, d = n, m = t; "crfrd" = Japan), and a number in B does not have its A value.

How it was read

  1. The key from the 1634 glosses. The glossed passages of the letter of 20 February 1634 gave 47 numbers their values. Frozen on 25 Nov 2026 (UTC).
  2. 1623, a test of the key. The frozen key decoded the 1623 letter on one pass: 32 of 54 words passed a word-list check (59.3%), under our own 65% line. Thirteen more values were then set from the sense, after that pass, and the margins of inv. 1079 were compared: they match.
  3. 1640 postscript, blind test 1. Transcribed from the bare copy, frozen, decoded once with the key, before the glossed copy was opened: 9 of 11 number tokens read (81.8%).
  4. 1643, blind test 2. The bare copy, frozen with its word rule, decoded once: 59 of 86 number groups covered by Dutch words (68.6%). The glossed copy, opened afterwards, agrees on 10 of 12 checkable runs.
  5. System B. A letter key built from the 1643 glosses alone, tested on the 1640 letter-cipher words, failed: 5 of 13 (38.5%). The fuller B key, built from the glosses of both years, has no held-out test.

Checks

  • Shuffled keys: 0 of 5,000 at each of two seeds for 1623, the 1640 postscript and 1643. For 1643, the same scorer on shuffled ciphertext also gave 0 of 5,000 at both seeds.
  • Blind second readings of the numbers (AI readers given the images only): 1640 56 of 57 groups agree; 1643 85 of 86; 1623, read by three non-Anthropic models, 121 of 124 and 127 of 127.
  • The repository's own statistical test on the 1623 letter: pass.
  • Exposure, stated in the record: the transcriber had the key in mind when copying the 1640 and 1643 numbers. The blind readers, who had no key, agree on all but one group in each.
  • Not yet done: the maintainers' review of the pull request, and any check by a person.

A word counts as read only when the key gives every letter with nothing supplied. Words resting on a gloss alone (system B, the code numerals, numbers with no value) are holes. The 1623 count uses the values set after the first pass, and its one hole is "kolden", read "konden". The test figures show the key works; they do not decide how much is read. The letters are read in part.

What remains uncertain

  • System B is read only through the glosses; its key has passed no held-out test.
  • 1643: 27 of 86 number groups are not covered; 12 are lone system-B signs the rule cannot score.
  • 1640: the 16 letter-cipher words of the postscript are open; the gloss is only partly legible at the archive's image size.
  • A letter of 7 March 1643 in the same series was found only as a glossed copy and was not transcribed.
  • The readings and the English have not been checked by a person or a reader of seventeenth-century Dutch.

Who did what

Malcolm McBratney (Brisbane) directed the work and set its rules, working with Claude (Anthropic): Claude Opus 5.5 sessions transcribed, built and tested the keys and assembled the folder; isolated Claude Fable sessions made the blind second readings of 1640 and 1643; three non-Anthropic models, run by Malcolm through Perplexity, read the 1623 numbers blind. He sent it as PR #996, still open; Daniel Bourdeau's first review led to the word counts above. The VOC clerks who glossed the copies made the key possible, and P. A. Leupe printed the 1643 passage first.

AI models made the transcriptions, keys, decodes, readings and English. No person has checked them against the images, and the maintainers have not yet reviewed the pull request in full.
