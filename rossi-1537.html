"I laugh [?so] much that I fear I shall burst": Pier Maria Rossi to Ferrante Gonzaga, November 1537

Two letters in a symbol cipher from the count of San Secondo to the viceroy of Sicily, mocking an Imperial commander's retreat in Piedmont. Read in part, with the key recovered from the ciphertext alone.

What is new and what was known

No edition or decipherment of these two letters was found, and the leaves carry no decipherment. Priority is not established: the search covered the Cyphersolver repository, the leaves themselves, the Estense records and six web searches (11 Nov 2026). The Gonzaga archive in Mantua, the Rossi family papers in Parma, the Rossi di San Secondo archive and the printed Gonzaga correspondence were not reached.

What this work adds: a key recovered from the ciphertext alone, a reading of 93% of the two letters, and an English translation.

The letters

The writer is Pier Maria III de' Rossi, count of San Secondo (1504-1547), married to Camilla Gonzaga. He writes to Ferrante Gonzaga, then viceroy of Sicily. The clear close of the first letter says why it is in cipher: "havendo pensato che a questa V. Ex.tia haverà havuta la zifra, ho scritto questa". Rossi had sent Ferrante the cipher and assumed it had arrived.

In October and November 1537 the French forced the pass of Susa and relieved Turin and Pinerolo; the Imperial commander raised the sieges and withdrew, and a truce followed in November. The letters fit that campaign. not checked against a history of the campaign

What Rossi writes

Italian as read; English from the translation in the repository. [?word] = doubtful; [...] = not read.

4 November: written "after supper"

He mocks an Imperial commander he calls "Astolfo", who had written all over Italy that he would fight and has instead withdrawn "to preserve an army": twenty miles the first time, forty to come.

"I am writing this after supper, so Your Excellency will [?hold] me excused if I am [...] brief, for besides having drunk very well of good muscatel and [?vernaccia], [?fra] l'Angiolino is waiting for me."

He recalls the departure from Genoa the previous December ("it was not a flight, or we would not have gone to the mole to embark"), stops himself, blames the wine, and promises day-by-day reports of Astolfo's "cowardices".

8 November: the second retreat

"Vi scrissi pur che la seconda retirata di questo poltrone di Astolfo serebe di quaranta miglia, et così fu, con perdita de xij pezi de artiglieria. Io mi rido ta[n]to che dubito scopiar como Marguto""I did write to you that the second retreat of this coward Astolfo would be of forty miles, and so it was, with the loss of 12 pieces of artillery. I laugh [?so] much that I fear I shall burst like Margutte."

Pinerolo and Turin are relieved, there is "not a farthing", and men from Mirandola have raided the Cremonese and sacked Piadena and a second place whose name is not read.

Who is "Astolfo"?

Astolfo is a mock name: the boastful paladin of Ariosto and Pulci (Margutte, who dies laughing, is Pulci's). From the retreats and the relief of Pinerolo and Turin, Astolfo is probably the Imperial commander in Piedmont, Alfonso d'Avalos, marchese del Vasto. unverified: the letters do not say so "Covos" is Francisco de los Cobos, the Emperor's secretary. Zopo, Vicencio the Venetian, Monfalconeto and "the gran Meo" are not identified.

The cipher

A simple substitution on an alphabet of invented signs: twenty letters, with extra signs (homophones) for a, i, c, g and u. No nulls or code words were seen; numbers are written in clear roman numerals.

One sign gave trouble. The first transcription gave a single code to a tall sign with a hat. On reading, it covers two letters, d (89 times) and t (152 times). The shapes differ slightly, but where a blind shape test and a second, non-blind look both gave d or t, they agreed on only 178 of 215 signs (82.8%). In practice d or t is chosen from the word around it.

How it was read

  1. 11 Nov 2026. Both letters transcribed from the Estense images, and the transcription frozen (hashed) before any decoding.
  2. A simulated-annealing solver scoring Italian four-letter sequences ran on the frozen transcription. It produced continuous Italian on its first run ("che Pinarolo et Turino sono socorsi et dodici pezi de artiglieria"). No crib and no gloss were used.
  3. Rare letters the solver had merged were completed by reading, and the reading aligned sign by sign to the frozen transcription.
  4. A second session, which saw only the page images, transcribed all 53 lines blind in its own sign names. It agrees with the first on 95.09% of aligned signs.

Checks

  • Shuffled keys: none of 5,000 shuffled keys scored as well as the real key, for either letter or both together.
  • Null controls: the same solver on scrambled versions of the cipher stream never reached Italian.
  • Maintainer's check: Daniel Bourdeau's own decoder reproduced the 39 lines sent with the issue, and he compared about 95 signs of letter B with the image. At integration (12 Nov) a separately written decoder reproduced all 53 lines, and about 70 signs of letter A verso were compared with the image.
  • Statistical validation: the repository's test calls the result "underdetermined": the reading scores far above random keys, but a solver can also fit a key of similar score. That is expected when a key is recovered by annealing, and it is not a pass.

What remains uncertain

  • d and t are chosen from context; the l sign has no blind confirmation.
  • Unread places in both letters, including a name and a place near Piadena (73 units), were looked at again on the image and left unread.
  • The identifications of Astolfo and the others are ours and unverified.
  • The reading of letter B was made with the key in hand.
  • Archives that may hold copies or replies (Mantua, Parma, the Rossi family archive) were not reached.
