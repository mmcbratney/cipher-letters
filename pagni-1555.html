"Our side will be deceived": an unnamed writer to Cristiano Pagni, undated

A full leaf of figure cipher sent to Cosimo I's secretary, on a league, the pledging of Cervia and Ravenna, and French dealings with a person hidden by a code. Read in part, with the key recovered from the ciphertext alone.

What is new and what was known

No edition or decipherment of this leaf was found. The archive's inventory (VII, p. 396 and n. 38) lists it as "In cifra", sender not named, undated, to Cristiano Pagni, and notes no decipherment. Priority is not established. Checked on 11-12 Nov 2026: the Cyphersolver repository; inventory VII pp. 385-398; the archive.org full text of Sauze, Romier II, Serristori's Legazioni and Desjardins III (OCR, not page by page); Calendar of State Papers Venice VI nos. 418-431; web searches. Not checked: the Medici Archive Project database (login), Cantagalli, Ancel, and the archive's Cifrari series (not digitised).

What this work adds: a key recovered from the ciphertext alone, a reading of 90.4% of the cipher units, an English translation, and a review of the catalogue date ("late March 1555"), which, as Daniel Bourdeau pointed out, looks wrong for the content; that rests on unfixed code values.

The letter

The recto has a line and a half in clear, then 26 lines of cipher. The verso has 10 lines of cipher and a dateline in clear. There is no decipherment on the leaf, no signature and no place.

c. 318v carries an outside address to Pagni, "Al my mag.co s.r il segr.o my / cristiano pagni my s.r / in florentia", with a seal remnant. The neighbouring leaves pair off so as to leave 315 with 318, so c. 318 is probably the address leaf of this letter. That is probable, not proved: no image shows the two leaves joined.

What the writer says

Italian as read; English from the translation in the repository. [?word] = a value proposed from context for a code sign; [n], [D] = code signs with no value; [...] = not read; a bracketed letter inside a word, or [?] after a word, is a letter the key does not give from the sign on the image.

The writer has stayed on at the request of the Marquis of Sarria to watch a negotiation with a person hidden by the code A. If the proposed value holds, A is the Pope. A friend gives him a warning.

"quel amico che intende parte delli segreti de Fran[z]e si mi dice c[h]iaramente che li nostri restara[n]no inga[n]nati""that friend who knows part of the secrets of France [?] tells me plainly [?] that our side will be [?] deceived [?]"

A league is being negotiated, with Cervia and Ravenna to be pledged to the Duke (code L) of Ferrara for money. "Monsignor di Lansa[c]", who came the evening before, presses it, and the Cardinal (code G) of Lorraine is expected. The writer suspects the French have already settled with A and are playing for time, to see whether Ottavio, with the troops they mean to raise, can force Siena (code S) to an accord with A as depositary.

"Breveme[n]te l'[?Papa] è inimicissimo de[l] [?Imperatore] e de[l] [?Duca]""In short [?], the [?Pope] is the bitterest enemy of the [?] [?Emperor] and of the [?] [?Duke]"
"Da la Mirandola vengano cavalli, da [P]arma parimente.""Horse are coming from Mirandola, and likewise from Parma [?]."

A says he will disarm, and the French will take on the soldiers. The writer does not know when he will be allowed to go to a place hidden as n, and asks to be kept in the good grace of L.

With the proposed code values this is a report from Rome on Franco-papal dealings against the Imperial side and Cosimo I under Paul IV. None of those values is fixed.

When was it written?

The leaf gives only day 20 of a month beginning with m (marzo or maggio). The filza is "Lettere del mese di Marzo 1555", but in March 1555 Julius III was dying, and if A is the Pope his hostility to the Emperor and the Duke does not fit him. Lansac reached Rome about 20 Oct 1555 and the Cardinal of Lorraine on 21 Nov 1555, which fits the persons, but no month of late 1555 begins with m. March 1556 fits the m but not Lansac and Lorraine, who were then in France. Lansac was in Rome again from 7 Sep 1556 to 5 Feb 1557. unverified: read in archive.org OCR So the date is late 1555 to early 1557, unresolved.

The cipher

Figures 1 to 9 and x make a paired alphabet: a bare figure and the same figure with one dot are neighbouring letters (1 a, 1' b; 2 c, 2. d; 3 e, 3' f; and so on to x u/v, x' z). A hooked 3 is also e. A repeat sign doubles the letter before it (49 units). Three word signs stand for che (23), in (13) and per (7 read).

Figures with two dots, a dot above the 2, a barred sign and a pi-like sign are nulls: 58 units, all between words or at a line end. Capital letters A, L, S, G, B, D, Q, small m, n and d, and an ampersand are codes: 23 units, none with a fixed value.

The pairing is weak in places. The dotted 4 that should be h is never read; where the text wants h the sign is the bare 4, so h is not shown by the key. The value b rests on eight words. A further 62 single-dotted signs, mostly t, l and b, stand between words and are dropped as nulls by context. They may be a separator class, or two-dot nulls with a dot not visible at 2000 px. All 62 are counted as not read.

How it was read

  1. 11 Nov 2026. Pass 1: a first transcription, 1,560 sign tokens. An annealing solver on it gave the vowels and no reading.
  2. Pass 2: the same reader again from new crops, without opening pass 1; 1,521 signs, frozen and stamped. The two passes agree on 82.0% of aligned positions. The key was read off the repeats of pass 2 (questo, quel, con, -stri), with no crib and no gloss, and the verso was then read on sight.
  3. Pass 3, not blind: every flagged unit looked at again on image tiles.
  4. A blind third transcription by another session that opened only the two images.
  5. Adjudication: the 138 units where pass 3 and the blind reader differ were each cropped with the label hidden and read before either transcription was looked at. Pass 3 was right at 60, the blind reader at 41, both readings possible at 34, neither at 3.

Checks

  • Shuffled keys: 0 of 5,000 shuffled keys reach the real key's score (on the first verifier's own decoder: real -2.262, best control -3.516).
  • Unaided solver: the contributor reports that a solver run on the pass-3 signs recovers 87.1% of the key and beats five frequency-preserving controls. The scripts are not in the folder and these were not re-run.
  • Statistical validation: the repository's test on the units read by the key gives a pass (reading -1.866 per letter, random keys -4.989, a key annealed onto shuffled ciphertext at best -2.959). It is a language-fit test of a key partly built from the same text.
  • Integration check, 12 Nov 2026: the build script rebuilds every file and figure byte for byte, and an independent decoder agrees with the key on all 1,369 units counted as read. Lines R03 and R04 (about 100 signs) were compared with the archive's image by the verifier: the digits and letters agree.
  • Maintainer's points: Daniel Bourdeau raised the tally of 69 signs dropped as nulls, the weak pairing for g/h and a/b, that the code values should stay marked as proposals, and the catalogue date. All are taken up in the files.

What remains uncertain

  • All eleven code values. The proposed ones (A Pope, L Duke, S Siena, G Cardinal, B Emperor) are from context only.
  • h, and the 62 signs dropped as nulls by context.
  • 8 unread signs, among them a looped sign three times at the start of a clause. The archive serves no image larger than 2000 px.
  • The date and the writer.
  • Whether c. 318 is the address leaf of this letter.
  • Apart from two lines, the leaf has not been compared with the image by a person.
