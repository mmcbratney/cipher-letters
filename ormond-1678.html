"Not credible": the Duke of Ormond to the Earl of Arran, 24 January 1677/8

Twenty figures set by a father as "a trial whether you are skilful in deciphering". They read in part with S. Tomokiyo's reconstruction of a 1680 Ormond key, once every figure is taken as 7 higher than in that key.

What is new and what was known

The key is Tomokiyo's. He reconstructed the 1680 Ormond-Longford cipher from the glosses in the HMC calendar and published it on his Cryptiana site (charlesii2.htm). There he also listed this passage as unidentified. A trial of his key at offset 0, left in a comment on that page, gave fragments, and he rejected it. Cryptoole lists it as XX1678-1, open.

Cyphersolver attempted the passage on 26-27 Oct 2026 and closed it unread: the figures fit neither Ormond key of these years that was tested, taken unchanged.

What this work adds: on 13 Nov 2026 Malcolm McBratney found that the 1678 figures are Tomokiyo's 1680 numbers plus 7, and checked it. By his record the offset is new. No reading of the passage was found before that date.

The letter

Ormond, Lord Lieutenant at Dublin, writes to his son Arran in London. The passage is about William Douglas, a Scottish nonconformist preacher brought to Lord Granard at Belfast in December 1677. Douglas told of a covenanting rising and hidden arms; searches in Edinburgh and Glasgow found nothing. Granard later calls him "a mountebank".

"I have had Mas. Douglas a week with me, but he is still a greater mystery to me ... If what he says of 445 and 342, be true 726, 91, 33, 425, 93, 58, 384, 54, 700, 720 but it seems 732, 573, 526, 32, 643, 214, 55, 440. This is a trial whether you are skilful in deciphering, else it might have been written in plain letters."

So the matter was minor, and the figures were a test of a cipher Arran held. Arran's reply of 5 February speaks of "this cipher ... which is betwixt us".

What it says

Reading from the repository. {..} = clear words of the letter; [figure] = no value; <..> = a value from the key's order or from context, not attested by a gloss. The English is AI-made.

"{If what he says of} [445] {and} [342], {be true} [726] and <King> are in <a> [700] [720]; {but it seems} to Ormond not credible."

The second run completes the clear words: Douglas's tale "seems to Ormond not credible". The first run reads only around its holes. The figures most likely say whom Douglas accused (445, 342) and what follows if he is believed.

The cipher

The 1680 key, as Tomokiyo reconstructed it, has two alphabets (23-46 and 47-70), then syllables and words in alphabetical order to about 840, then names. In the 1678 letter every number is 7 higher: the five low figures 32, 33, 54, 55 and 58 are the single letters c, d, a, b and e.

The figures were checked on the scan of the printed page. The sixth figure of the first run is printed 58, not 57; "57" came from Tomokiyo's quotation and went into the catalogue entry from there.

How it was read

  1. 26-27 Oct 2026. The 1678 two-letter code of Ormond and Arran was ruled out (it uses letter pairs, not figures). The 1680 Longford key taken unchanged gave only consonant runs, as Tomokiyo had found.
  2. 13 Nov 2026. An agent Malcolm McBratney ran on Perplexity first noticed the offset. It was then checked independently: transcription, glosses on the page images, controls rebuilt.
  3. Each 1680 value used was checked on the page images of HMC New Series v (pp.454, 469, 479, 486, 498).

Checks

  • Offsets: over offsets -150 to +150 on the Longford keys 1 and 2 (602 key-offset trials), only -7 on key 1 spells any word of 5 or more letters across 3 or more groups ("credible").
  • Random figures: random sets of the same shape, at their best of 602 trials, reach 8 letters 6 times in 2,000; shuffles of the real figures at -7 reach it 0 times in 3,000. The bare hit count is weak: 14 of 20 was reached in 5 of 2,000 random sets.
  • Integration check (13 Nov 2026): the scripts were re-run and reproduced every figure. A decoder typed afresh from Tomokiyo's table image gave the same 14 values. A control with another word list gave 8+ letters in 0 of 1,000 random sets. The word statistic was chosen after "credible" had been seen, so these margins are supporting evidence, not a pre-registered test.
  • Correction: on p.486 the print puts "and" over 84 alone, not over 84 50. Reading 84 as "an" is the contributor's; the group gives "and" either way.

By the read bar, values from order or context count as holes: 10 of 20 = 50%. The contributor's count, which takes them as read, is 15 of 20 = 75%. Tomokiyo's key as published gives 14 of 20.

What remains uncertain

  • Five figures have no value: no gloss for their numbers anywhere in HMC New Series v, and none in Tomokiyo's table.
  • Whether +7 holds over the whole table is not known. There are only 20 figures, none repeated, and no other passage in the 1678 numbering.
  • The manuscript has not been seen. A gloss on the sent letter or Ormond's copy might exist in the NLI volumes (not digitised) or in Arran's papers in the Bodleian Carte MSS (MS. Carte 50 fols 439-440, reported as a January 1678 Ormond-Arran cipher, not seen).
